# Research workbench: assets and maintenance

The desk models, signal animation, page layout, and interaction code are original implementations for this project. The workbench takes visual inspiration from [Zhuoyuan Li's studio](https://zhuoyuan.li/studio); no code, models, or artwork from that website are included.

## Third-party components

- **Three.js 0.180.0**: `js/vendor/three/three.module.js` and `three.core.js`. MIT license, copyright Three.js Authors. The complete license is retained in `js/vendor/three/LICENSE`. Upstream: <https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/r180>. Files obtained from the versioned npm distribution through jsDelivr. Both modules are served locally, with no runtime CDN dependency.
- **Natural Earth, 1:110m Admin 0 – Countries**: `assets/studio/countries.geojson`. Public-domain geographic data under the [Natural Earth terms of use](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/). Source: <https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/master/geojson/ne_110m_admin_0_countries.geojson>. The local copy retains country names and geometry, removes unused attributes, and rounds coordinates to three decimal places for this small decorative globe. It is not a navigation or boundary reference.
- The ECG waveform is synthesized from Gaussian P/QRS/T components; it contains no patient data and does not perform medical measurements.

## Updating the studio

- Entry plays multilingual greetings followed by a camera reveal of the bench. Skip, Escape, direct atlas links, returning from the atlas, and reduced motion bypass the introduction. `js/studio-entrance.js` owns this sequence; `js/studio-animation.js` supplies cancellable animations.
- The computer, globe, and envelope use actual Three.js camera flights, including when ambient motion is paused. The monitor content follows the projected screen bounds; the envelope leads to an overhead stationery page. Returning restores the original bench pose and fixed-foot height framing. A Cancel control handles interrupted camera flights; unavailable WebGL uses HTML panels.
- `js/studio-letter.js` controls the stationery sequence: three paper panels fold, enter an envelope, and receive a wax seal. Drag the stamp onto the wax, or use the keyboard / stamp button. An original inline SVG pigeon picks up the envelope and flies away. This is a visual sequence: the final mailto link still requires the visitor to confirm delivery in their own email application. It always targets `xidong03@163.com`. Closing a busy animation cancels it safely, preserves the draft, and restores a stable state; reduced motion skips the movement. Clipboard failure reveals selectable letter text.

- Entry page: `studio/index.html`; styling: `css/studio.css`.
- Project links and descriptions are accessible HTML in the project dialog. Keep them consistent with the publications on `index.html`.
- Education descriptions are in `educationPlaces` in `js/studio.js`. Singapore / NUS is the current PhD location, confirmed by the author; Shenyang and Dundee represent previous undergraduate joint training. The travel atlas combines those descriptions with the author's supplied photo folders. Reset / Home returns to Singapore.
- The author supplied the NUS logo (`assets/img/logo/NUS.jpg`) and confirmed the education details: NUS PhD, 2027–present; both undergraduate degrees are Bachelor of Engineering in Biomedical Engineering, 2022–2026; Northeastern grade 88/100; Dundee First Class Honours. Keep the homepage, bilingual catalog, and studio location descriptions consistent. Education logos share one grid column and preserve their aspect ratios; CSS handles the NUS file's white margins without changing the supplied image.
- Desk geometry and synthetic signal drawing are in `js/studio-scene.js`; the physical atlas, stand, tilted meridian, pins and globe controls are in `js/travel-globe.js`. Both globes share a locally generated CanvasTexture from the GeoJSON. Nearby pins have separated heads with stems anchored to their actual coordinates, so destinations such as Rome and Vatican City can both be selected.
- Serve the repository over HTTP and visit `/studio/`. `/studio/globe/` opens the atlas directly; the legacy `/studio/#atlas` entry also works. No package installation is required. `studio/globe/index.html` is generated from the bench entry; regenerate it after changing `studio/index.html` with `python tools/build_travel_atlas.py`.
- Travel photographs in `travel/` are the author's unchanged originals: 20 places and 59 photographs, including the three Warsaw photographs. `tools/build_travel_atlas.py` owns bilingual place names, countries and approximate place coordinates, and generates `js/travel-data.js`. Coordinates represent named cities or regions, not exact photograph locations. No capture dates or trip narratives are inferred. Add photos to existing folders and rerun the builder; new folders need a metadata row first.
- Clicking the desk globe expands it into the atlas. Clicking a pin or a destination in the place list reveals a taped note and a photo print; arrows and thumbnails navigate the selected album, and clicking the print opens a full-size viewer with arrow-key navigation. Only the selected album is requested; thumbnails are lazy-loaded. The atlas includes its own language/theme controls and Singapore clock. Reduced motion skips the expansion animation. HTML destination/gallery controls remain available without WebGL.
- Controls below the scene provide keyboard-accessible alternatives to raycasting. The globe supports drag, pinch, wheel, arrow keys, `+` / `-`, and `Home`.
- Picking uses mesh surfaces only; decorative outlines cannot steal clicks from adjacent objects. The computer, globe, notebook, and keyboard have separate scene groups and actions. Noninteractive surfaces block selection of hidden objects.
- The keyboard opens a small client-side terminal with `help`, `projects`, `globe`, `notes`, `email`, and `clear` commands. These only navigate the workbench; they do not execute shell commands.
- The envelope opens a letter composer addressed to `xidong03@163.com`. Subject and body are URL-encoded into a `mailto:` link after folding the letter. The visitor reviews and sends in their own email application. There is no mail backend or automatic delivery. Drafts remain in the page when closing/reopening the dialog and are discarded on reload. The Copy letter option includes a manual-copy fallback.
- The studio shares `js/preferences-init.js`, `js/preferences.js`, and `css/preferences.css` with the academic homepage. Preferences use `xw.theme` and `xw.language` in localStorage; blocked storage still permits changes within the current page. A default theme follows the system until the visitor makes an explicit choice. Palette changes also adjust the desk and globe lighting. UI translations are in the shared bilingual catalog; typed letters and terminal history retain their original text.
- The standing desk has a 75–115 cm range, sitting/standing presets, hold-to-adjust arrows, and a display that toggles presets or stops preset movement. HTML and 3D arrows move continuously while pressed and stop on release/cancellation; HTML arrows support holding Space/Enter. Blur, hidden tabs, dialogs, and context loss stop held movement. The desktop, crossbar, objects, and cables move together; feet and leg sleeves stay fixed while the inner columns extend. The camera framing depends only on viewport dimensions and fits the entire height range, so the feet retain their screen position and the desk never zooms while adjusting height. Explicit adjustments work with ambient motion paused. Reduced motion applies presets immediately; held input directly controls height.
- All eight desk objects, including the circuit board, react to pointer hover or focus on their corresponding HTML buttons. Each has a small lift/tilt and stronger edge/material highlights; reduced motion keeps highlights without moving the objects. The board opens the research projects. Mesh-only raycasting continues to respect occlusion and ignore decorative outlines.
- The clock uses `Intl.DateTimeFormat` with the fixed `Asia/Singapore` timezone and updates every second, independently of the visitor's timezone. Background tabs stop the clock timer as well as the 3D loop.
- System reduced-motion preferences pause the desk on first load; explicit Resume motion re-enables it. Background tabs stop the rendering loop. Lack of WebGL falls back to an SVG illustration and HTML research/location controls; lack of JavaScript preserves the illustration and research links.
